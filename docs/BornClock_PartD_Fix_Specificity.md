# BornClock — Part D-Fix: Genuinely Personal, Chart-Grounded Readings
## Single Claude Code session prompt.

---

## CONTEXT FOR CLAUDE CODE

Part D (reading UX) is built, tested, and deployed to staging — but the
person who reviewed the actual output on staging found it **too
generic**. This session's job is to fix that specifically, not to
rebuild the feature.

**The person running this session does not read code.** All
verification must come back as real generated text they can read and
judge for specificity themselves.

### The exact problem, with a real example

Here is an actual reading generated by the current system (paste
verbatim, this is the thing to fix):

> "In your professional life, you may feel drawn toward roles where
> communication, analytical insight, and steady craftsmanship are
> valued... During this time, work life often benefits most from quiet
> consistency and patience rather than rushed milestones."

This is a problem because it is **true for almost any chart** — it
never names a specific planet, house, sign placement, strength, or
aspect that makes THIS chart's career picture different from another
chart's. A good reading for the same person should read more like:

> "Your 10th house of career sits in Vrischika, ruled by Mars, and
> Mars itself sits strongly placed in your 1st house — giving you a
> career path where personal drive and direct action matter more than
> smooth diplomacy. Saturn's aspect on your 10th house suggests
> recognition builds slowly rather than quickly, often after age 32-35.
> Mercury, your house-of-communication significator, sits in Kanya in
> your 2nd house — well-placed for financial or analytical work
> specifically, more than purely social or creative career paths."

Notice the difference: real chart facts (which sign is in the 10th
house, which planet sits where, aspects, approximate timing from Dasha)
versus generic astrology-flavored language that could apply to anyone.

### Root cause

The current Gemini prompt asks for readings "grounded in relevant chart
data" but does not specify exactly which data fields must be referenced,
in how much detail, or check afterward whether the output is actually
specific versus generic. This session fixes both the prompt AND adds an
automated specificity check, the same way Part D already has an
automated safety check.

## PART 1: REWRITE THE GENERATION PROMPTS

For EACH of the 5 reading sections (Snapshot, Life Areas, Right Now For
You, Doshas, Deeper Chart Layers), rewrite the Gemini prompt to
REQUIRE — not just suggest — specific chart facts:

### 1.1 Minimum required facts per section

- **Career**: must reference the 10th house sign AND its ruling planet's placement (house/sign/strength), AND at least one other planet relevant to career (Sun for authority, Mercury for communication/trade, Saturn for discipline/delay), AND connect it to the current Dasha lord if relevant.
- **Relationships**: must reference the 7th house sign and ruler, Venus's placement (sign/house/strength), and Navamsa Moon or Navamsa Venus sign if available.
- **Health**: must reference the 6th house (ailments/obstacles), the Lagna lord's strength, and any planet in a health-relevant house — while still respecting the existing "no medical claims" safety rule from the original Part D build (specific chart facts, not specific diagnoses).
- **Money**: must reference the 2nd and 11th house signs/rulers, and Jupiter's placement (traditional wealth significator).
- **Family**: must reference the 4th house (mother/home) and 9th house (father/fortune) signs and rulers.
- **Right now for you**: must reference the CURRENT Dasha lord's actual house placement, sign, and Shadbala strength (strong/weak) in THIS chart — not a generic description of what that planet's periods mean in textbooks.
- **Doshas**: keep the existing calm, non-fear-based tone rules from the original build, but add the specific house/planet causing the dosha (e.g., "Mars in your 2nd house" not just "Mangal Dosha is present").
- **Deeper chart layers**: must name at least 2 actual divisional charts by their real computed sign placements (e.g., "In your Navamsa, your Moon falls in Simha" not just "your complementary patterns suggest...").

### 1.2 Explicitly instruct Gemini to avoid generic phrasing

Add to every prompt: "Do not write sentences that would be equally true
for a different birth chart. Every sentence should reference a specific
planet, house, sign, or computed value from the data provided. Avoid
generic astrology phrases like 'you may feel drawn toward' or 'this is
a time for' without immediately connecting them to a specific chart
placement."

### 1.3 Pass more of the validated engine's data into the prompt

Check `calculateBirthChart()`'s full output (per Part B) and confirm the
prompt-building code is actually passing through: all 9 planets' sign
AND house AND retrograde/combust status, not just Sun/Moon; at least
Navamsa (D9) for all planets, not just mentioned vaguely; Shadbala
strength category (strong/weak) per planet if available; house lords
for houses 1,2,4,6,7,9,10,11 (the ones referenced above). If any of
this data is computed by the engine but NOT currently being passed into
the Gemini prompt, that is the root cause — fix the data-passing code,
not just the prompt wording.

## PART 1.5: GUARD AGAINST HALLUCINATED SPECIFICITY (RESEARCH-BACKED ADDITION)

Real research on AI astrology products found a serious, well-documented
failure mode worse than generic writing: **a model can sound highly
specific while inventing wrong facts** ("uniform confidence" hides which
values are fabricated — a wrong Ascendant gets stated in the same
confident tone as a correct outer planet). A generic reading is boring;
a confidently WRONG specific reading actively destroys trust once a user
notices the planet named isn't actually where their real chart places it.

This means Part 2's specificity check is not sufficient alone — it must
be paired with a **factual accuracy check**. Build this concretely, not
as a vague instruction:

1. Build a small structured-extraction step (this can itself be a
   focused Gemini call, or simple pattern-matching if reliable enough —
   your call, but document which you chose and why) that pulls out every
   specific chart claim the generated reading makes: which planet, which
   house, which sign, in what combination.
2. For each extracted claim, compare it against the ACTUAL value from
   that exact chart's real `calculateBirthChart()` output.
3. If ANY claim doesn't match the real data, that generation FAILS this
   check — regardless of whether it also passed the Part 2 specificity
   check. Retry the generation. If it fails twice, flag it rather than
   silently shipping it.
4. Report, with real numbers: out of the test charts generated, how many
   individual claims were extracted and checked, how many matched, how
   many were hallucinated (and paste the exact hallucinated claim
   alongside the real correct value, so a human can see the gap directly).
5. Also apply the "synthesis, not just listing" bar from real competitor
   products: a good section should connect at least TWO chart elements
   to each other and to a real-world implication (e.g., "your 7th house
   lord X sits in Y, [creating/challenging] Z" — not just "Venus is in
   Kanya" stated in isolation with no connection drawn).

## PART 2: BUILD AN AUTOMATED SPECIFICITY CHECK

This is as important as the safety-language check already in place.
Build a test that:

1. Generates a reading for the reference chart (1988-11-05, Delhi) AND at least 2 other very different charts.
2. For each section, checks that the generated text contains at least [N, e.g. 3-4] specific chart terms (planet names, house numbers, sign names, or "Navamsa"/"Shadbala"/dosha names) — not just checking word count, checking for REFERENCE to real computed values.
3. Cross-check: pull the actual computed chart values for planets 10th-house-lord, 7th-house-lord, etc. for the test chart, and confirm those SPECIFIC values (not just "a planet") appear somewhere in the relevant section's text.
4. If a section fails this check, that's a real bug — retry the generation once with a stronger prompt reminder, and if it still fails, flag it (don't silently ship generic text).

Report the actual pass/fail count and, for any failures, paste the
generic text that triggered the failure.
